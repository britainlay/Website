FINANCIAL LITERACY / 01

Budgeting.

Build a plan for your money before your money decides where to go. Budgeting isn't about restricting yourself. It's about understanding your money and making intentional decisions with it.

01 — THE BASICS

What is budgeting?

Budgeting is creating a plan for how you will use your money. Instead of wondering where your money went at the end of the month, a budget helps you decide where it should go before you spend it.

$

Income

Money coming in, such as wages, an internship paycheck, allowance, or other legitimate sources of income.

↓

Expenses

Money going out for things like transportation, food, subscriptions, entertainment, and other expenses.

→

Goals

What you want your money to help you accomplish, such as saving, investing, education, or future purchases.

02 — WHY IT MATTERS

Why should I care?

A budget isn't just a spreadsheet. It can help you understand your habits and make decisions based on your priorities.

Control

A budget helps you understand where your money is going instead of letting spending happen automatically.

Goals

Saving for something becomes easier when you give your money a specific purpose.

Preparedness

Unexpected expenses can happen. Having money set aside can make them easier to handle.

Awareness

Tracking your spending can reveal habits you might not notice otherwise.

03 — GET STARTED

How to build a budget.

You don't need a complicated spreadsheet to start. Begin with understanding your income, expenses, and priorities.

01

Know your income

Start by figuring out how much money you actually have coming in during a typical month.

  • Part-time work
  • Internships
  • Allowance
  • Other legitimate income
02

Track your expenses

Look at where your money is actually going. Don't guess. Use your bank statements, receipts, or a budgeting app.

  • Needs
  • Wants
  • Subscriptions
  • Transportation
03

Set your priorities

Once you understand your expenses, decide what matters most to you.

  • Essential expenses
  • Savings
  • Financial goals
  • Discretionary spending
04

Review and adjust

A budget doesn't have to be perfect. Review what actually happened and adjust your plan when your situation changes.

05 — MY PERSPECTIVE

My take.

Why I believe budgeting is a skill worth learning early.

I believe students should learn budgeting because it is a key component to success in life. I've started tracking my money, saving, and thinking more about budgeting myself, and I've realized that understanding your money is about more than just knowing how much you have. It's about building a foundation that can support your future goals.

01 — Build a foundation I believe everything needs a foundational system to succeed, and personal finances are no different.
02 — Start early You don't have to wait until you're an adult to start learning how to manage money.
03 — Think beyond today Budgeting isn't just about today's spending. It's about creating habits that can support future goals.
06 — WATCH OUT FOR THESE

Common mistakes.

MISTAKE 01

Ignoring small purchases

Small purchases may seem insignificant individually, but tracking them can help you understand your overall spending habits.

MISTAKE 02

Never checking your budget

A budget only helps if you compare your plan with what you're actually spending.

MISTAKE 03

Making an unrealistic plan

If your budget doesn't reflect your actual life, it becomes difficult to maintain.

MISTAKE 04

Forgetting irregular expenses

School expenses, gifts, maintenance, annual subscriptions, and unexpected costs can all affect your plan.
