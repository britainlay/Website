Investing.

Understanding risk, research, and long-term growth — and how my own experience has shaped the way I think about investing.

01 — THE BASICS

What is investing?

Investing means putting money or other resources into something with the expectation that it may increase in value or generate income over time. Unlike saving, investing involves risk, and returns are never guaranteed.

Stocks

A stock represents ownership in a company. Investors may benefit if the company's value increases, but the investment can also lose value.

Bonds

Bonds generally represent lending money to an issuer in exchange for interest payments and repayment under specified terms.

Funds

Funds can hold collections of investments, allowing investors to gain exposure to multiple assets through one investment.

03 — RISK & REWARD

Risk matters.

Investing always involves uncertainty. Understanding how much risk you're taking is just as important as thinking about potential returns.

Potential return

Investments can potentially grow in value or generate income, but higher potential returns generally come with greater uncertainty.

Potential returns are not guaranteed.

The risk you take

The value of an investment can fall as well as rise. Understanding volatility and the possibility of loss is an important part of making informed decisions.

Past performance does not guarantee future results.
04 — DIVERSIFICATION

Don't depend on one investment.

Stocks Ownership
Bonds Debt
Funds Diversified exposure
Cash Liquidity

Diversification means spreading investments across different assets rather than relying entirely on one.

The goal is not to eliminate risk. Instead, diversification can help reduce the impact that one investment may have on an overall portfolio.

05 — LONG-TERM THINKING

Investing isn't just about today.

Think beyond the next market move.

Investing can involve looking beyond short-term price changes and thinking about goals, time horizons, risk, and strategy. There will always be uncertainty in financial markets, which is why having a foundation and a plan matters.

Where should you start?

Learn before you leap.

If you're interested in investing, I think the best place to start is by learning the basics. 11 — MISCONCEPTIONS

What investing isn't.

MISCONCEPTION

"You need to be rich to invest."

Investing can involve different amounts of money and different levels of risk. Learning about investing doesn't require being wealthy.

MISCONCEPTION

"Investing is a quick way to get rich."

Markets can move unpredictably, and investments can lose value. Long-term financial planning is more complicated than chasing quick returns.

MISCONCEPTION

"Someone online knows exactly what will happen."

Financial content online should be evaluated carefully. Nobody can guarantee how a particular investment will perform.

MISCONCEPTION

"You have to know everything first."

Learning is a process. Building a foundation and gradually expanding your knowledge can be more useful than waiting until you feel like an expert.
