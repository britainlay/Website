FINANCIAL LITERACY / 05

Financial Awareness.

Financial literacy isn't just about knowing how money works. It's about understanding your choices, building a system, and making informed decisions.

01 — MY STARTING POINT

I knew I should save. I didn't know much beyond that.

Where I started

When I first started thinking about personal finance, I wasn't very financially aware. I knew that saving money was important, but I didn't fully understand everything that went into managing money effectively.

Where I am now

Learning about budgeting, credit, investing, and compound growth has helped me understand how different parts of personal finance connect with each other.

02 — STUDENT FINANCE

Financial decisions start earlier than we think.

I think students face many of the same financial misunderstandings that adults do, but often without having enough opportunities to learn about them beforehand.

01

Not tracking spending

02

Misunderstanding credit

Credit isn't free money. Understanding how borrowing works matters.

03

Investing misconceptions

Investing can seem intimidating when you don't understand the basics.

04

Wanting things immediately

Understanding needs, wants, and opportunity costs can change spending decisions.

05

Not understanding interest

Interest can work for you or against you depending on the situation.

06

Limited financial education

Students deserve opportunities to learn practical financial skills before entering adulthood.

A financial foundation starts with a system.

Give your money a direction.

One of the biggest things I've learned is that having a system can make it easier to understand where your money is going and how your decisions connect to your goals.

04 — SPENDING

Think before you spend.

Spending isn't automatically bad. The important part is understanding whether a purchase fits into the financial system you've created.

Can I afford it?

Does my budget actually have room for this purchase without interfering with my other goals?

Do I actually need it?

Is this something I need, or is it something I simply want right now?

Does it affect my goals?

Could this purchase make it harder to reach something I'm saving or planning for?

Can I spend it guilt-free?

If your budget gives you room for something you enjoy, spending money doesn't have to feel wrong.

Guilt-free spending can still be part of a good budget. A budget should help you control your money, not make you afraid to use it.

05 — PROTECT YOUR MONEY

Not everything online deserves your trust.

Financial awareness also means knowing when something doesn't seem right and taking time to investigate before making a decision.

01

Personal information

Be careful when someone asks for sensitive personal or financial information, especially unexpectedly.

02

Too good to be true

Promises that seem unrealistic should make you stop and question the opportunity before acting.

03

False statements

Don't assume something is legitimate simply because someone makes confident claims about it.

I've come across suspicious financial opportunities before. What made them stand out to me were requests for personal information, claims that seemed too good to be true, and statements that didn't seem legitimate.

06 — MY PERSPECTIVE

Make more money. Manage it better.

I believe both making money and learning how to manage it are important. But being able to manage the money you're making is key.

Understand your money Know where your money is going and what resources you have available.
Use your resources Think about how your resources can help you reach your goals.
Build toward your goals Financial awareness can help connect today's decisions with future goals.

Becoming more financially aware has helped me understand where my money is going, how I can better utilize my resources, and how those decisions can help me reach my goals.

07 — LOOKING BACK

What I would tell my freshman-year self.

Stay consistent. Adapt when necessary.

I would tell my freshman-year self to be consistent and go with the original plan. If it doesn't work, adjust it and continue forward. Rinse and repeat. I've learned that adapting and improving isn't just useful for finances. It can apply to almost everything in life.

08 — YOUR TURN

It's never too late to start.

I want other students to realize that it's never too late to start learning and applying financial literacy. You don't need to know everything at the beginning. Start with the basics, build a system, learn from your mistakes, adjust when necessary, and keep moving forward.

Learn → Build → Apply → Adapt → Grow
