Credit.

Understanding credit, building financial habits, and learning how responsible borrowing can become part of a strong financial foundation.

01 — THE BASICS

What is credit?

Credit allows someone to borrow money or access something of value with an agreement to repay it later, usually under specific terms.

My first understanding of credit

When I was younger, I actually thought a debit card was a card you used to borrow money. Once I started learning more about personal finance, I understood the difference between debit and credit and learned that credit can provide benefits when it is used responsibly.

THE FOUNDATION

Credit isn't free money.

Understanding that borrowed money still has to be repaid is one of the most important ideas I think students should learn before using credit.

02 — CREDIT SCORES

What affects your credit?

A credit score is influenced by several parts of your credit history. These are four areas I think are especially important for students to understand.

01

Payment History

Whether you make payments on time is an important part of your credit history.

02

Credit Utilization

Credit utilization looks at how much of your available revolving credit you are using.

03

Credit History

The length and history of your credit accounts can be part of how your credit profile is evaluated.

04

New Credit

Opening multiple new credit accounts or applying for credit frequently can affect your credit profile.

03 — CREDIT CARDS & INTEREST

Borrowing comes with responsibility.

Credit is not free money.

One of the things I think students should understand early is that using credit doesn't mean you have extra money. One of my biggest concerns with credit is spending more than planned and dealing with high interest. Understanding what you're borrowing and having a plan to repay it can help create better financial habits.

Spending Know what you're actually able to afford before making a purchase.
Interest Understand that borrowing can become more expensive depending on the terms.
Repayment Have a plan for paying what you borrow according to the account terms.
04 — BUILDING A FOUNDATION

Credit doesn't have to be bad.

I used to think credit was something that could easily become a problem. What I wish I had understood earlier is that having credit is not necessarily bad as long as you can pay it off and have a foundational structure. To me, that means understanding what you're using, knowing what you can afford, and developing responsible financial habits.

06 — CREDIT FOR STUDENTS

Should teenagers learn about credit?

Yes, I believe teenagers should learn about credit and potentially use credit products responsibly because there can be benefits to building financial knowledge early. The important part is understanding how credit works and using it wisely.

Learn first Understand the basics before making financial decisions.
Don't treat credit like cash Remember that borrowed money still has to be repaid.
Build a foundation Develop habits around budgeting, spending, saving, and repayment.
07 — FOR STUDENTS

Start learning before you need it.

You don't have to learn everything about credit from one place. I recommend using different sources and learning from people you trust.

Learn from different perspectives.

Use online resources, books, family members, and other people around you who have experience with personal finance. The goal isn't to become an expert immediately. The goal is to start building a foundation that you can continue developing over time.
